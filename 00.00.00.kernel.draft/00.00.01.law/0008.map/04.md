## Relationships

source: [01 / Relationships](01.md#relationships); [02 / Relationships](02.md#relationships)

why: Your example can have both a broader grouping and an immediate predecessor without corrupting the container address.

The typed rows are member-of for idea grouping, continues-from for an immediate work chain, and references for an ordinary dependency or a pointer. These labels describe connections and grant no authority. A structural parent still requires its existing lawful proof. No global single-parent rule is inferred for ideas. An owner row is a different record. It carries the address of who authorized the law. A desk may authorize the desks under it. The child records that address on face 08. The address gains no new digit.

The compact form selects the useful handle: member-of, continues-from, and references are different labels. None of them is a parent proof. An owner row carries the address of who authorized the law.

## Resolution

source: [01 / Resolution](01.md#resolution); [02 / Resolution](02.md#resolution)

why: An address locates a thing; a relationship explains why the thing is relevant.

Resolve an address with the existing syntax walk and seqaddr grammar. Then inspect the named relationship or subject section. Do not walk every reference as a parent, scan sideways to guess a missing source, or choose by numerical proximity. An explicit source link may be followed for the named retrieval job; that is not an authority climb.

The compact form selects the useful handle: Locate -> verify identity -> inspect relation. Authority climb uses its own proof.

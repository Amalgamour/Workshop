## Job

owner: kernel-only

Face 02 supplies working forms of a named subject section. A card is read for a quick use. A template builds a new artifact. A schema checks an artifact. A payload is code or configuration that the face declares and binds. These kinds are not interchangeable: a card is not a template, and a schema is not permission. A form does not add normative conditions absent from face 01.

## Coverage

owner: kernel-only

Create a face 02 section when a working form serves the job. The face 02 section heading is the exact face 01 heading it represents. A card that is shorter than the rule stays. A card whose only extra sentence is to read the source comes off. A template is complete enough to build its artifact, and a schema to check one; neither is held to shortness. If no form is needed, its face is absent and the menu omits it.

## Forms

owner: kernel-only

One face 02 section may hold several forms of its subject. Each form declares its name, kind, purpose, governing source section, and revision. A form name is unique within its section. It is a label inside the section, not a heading and not an address axis. Face 03 lists each form by name, kind, and purpose. Work selects a form by its name. Do not select by kind alone, by the newest file, or by guess. A selection that matches no form, or more than one, is refused.

## Agreement

owner: kernel-only

Preserve the governing conditions, exceptions, and limits in the compact form. A card that changes a condition is a conflict even when its heading matches. Report that conflict with both source cuts. Read the actual governing file before a consequential write. Governing conditions, exceptions, and limits outrank shortness. A card may be shorter. A shorter card that drops a limit or adds a condition is a conflict. Shortness does not win. Every form kind is held to the same test. A conflict between a form and its governing source goes to the Director. Intelligence names and explains the conflict. It does not choose which source to follow, amend the source, or waive a check. The act that depends on the form waits for the answer.

## Requirements

owner: kernel-only

A form requirement has two parts, and each names its ground. The meaning part says what must be present, allowed, or done. Its ground is the governing face 01 section, or a recorded task grant that applies to the work. The representation part says how that meaning is written: a field, a type, an order, a count, or an encoding. Its ground is the form's own declaration. Meaning present in prose but absent from its field fails the representation. Meaning absent everywhere fails the meaning, and only when a ground requires it. A representation must not narrow the meanings its source allows, add a permission or an approval gate, or require a meaning that no ground requires. Compute checks the shapes and checks that each named ground exists. Intelligence judges whether the ground supports the requirement. A requirement without a ground, or with a ground that does not support it, is a conflict under Agreement.

## Fields

owner: kernel-only

This section applies to templates and schemas. Each field declares its name and job, its shape, whether it is required or optional, which answers are valid, and who supplies it. The answers are: not supplied, null, empty, a value, and not applicable. A field declares which of them it accepts and how each is written. Compute keeps them distinct and never turns one into another. Intelligence supplies meaning, including the choice of a semantic reference: which source, page, person, or dependency is meant. Compute resolves that reference and refuses one that does not resolve. Compute generates the output location, numbering, identity, stamp, dates of record, hashes, and encoding. A declared source supplies a retrieved value. Compute does not invent a missing meaning; it asks for it. An example value shows shape. It is not a decision and not a default. A repeating block exists only where the form declares it, with the identity scheme compute assigns to its items.

## Binding

owner: kernel-only

Work binds the exact revision of the form it selected and the bytes of each source the form declares. The selection is explicit: the seated face, or a revision the work names. A newer draft does not replace a selected revision, and a version number does not make one revision superior to another. When bound bytes change, the binding is stale until it is checked again. A preserved copy stays valid evidence of what was used; it is reported as history, not as current. In this development phase, a changed binding, conflicting applicability, or a proposed replacement of stable governing work goes to the Director. A form declares what it depends on. Compute resolves declared dependencies and reports any that are missing, stale, ambiguous, or conditional. It does not infer an undeclared dependency, and a hash does not prove compatibility or meaning. A relationship between pages is recorded on face 08.

## Construction

owner: kernel-only

This section applies to templates. Compute builds the whole artifact from the form and the supplied meaning and shows the whole artifact for review. Text is read and written in the encoding the form declares, never in a platform default. A raw source is kept byte for byte. A serialization step the form declares is a change of representation, not of meaning. A failed check names the field, the expected and the observed shape, keeps the supplied input, and gives the correction route. Nothing supplied is silently dropped, re-encoded, or substituted. A refused input is not a rejected idea. A review binds the candidate it judged. When recording the review changes the artifact, the candidate and the final artifact each keep their own identity. A changed form, source, dependency, or supplied value makes the earlier review stale. Building, checking, and review do not sign, approve, seat, lock, or release. Those acts stay with their own laws.

## Payload

owner: kernel-only

A payload is a file beside its face. The face declares it by name, kind, revision, the bytes it binds, the runner or reader that may load it, and how it is checked. The face is the governing form; the payload file is not a face. The declaring face is the payload's source and its lifecycle. A file extension, a declared runner, or a payload kind grants no permission to run. Running stays with the law that owns that act. Each face remains a logical addressed object; how faces and payloads are stored is a representation, and a later store keeps these declarations.

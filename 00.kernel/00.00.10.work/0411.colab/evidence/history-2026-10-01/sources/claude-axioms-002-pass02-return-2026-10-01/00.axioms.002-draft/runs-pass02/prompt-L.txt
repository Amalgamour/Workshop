=== AXIOMS ===

## 1. Build Fractally
Structure should repeat so that change can remain local.
A system should use the same relationships at different scales wherever possible.
Local work should have a known parent, boundary, state, authority, and route.
A change in one lawful projection should not require rewriting unrelated parts of the system.

Why: Large systems fail when every change has unknown global consequences. Fractal structure makes change bounded, repairable, and testable.

Protects: maintainability, locality, upgradeability, scale.

## 2. Recover the Why Before Expensive Commitment
Search for why in proportion to the cost of being wrong about it.
Routine, low-consequence work should not be delayed by unnecessary causal investigation.
When misunderstanding the reason could materially change the design, outcome, safety, authority, or future cost, recover the why before committing.
Stop when additional why is unlikely to change the decision.
If the required why cannot be recovered, keep it unknown or inferred at the proper state; do not manufacture an authoritative reason to make the work feel complete.

Why: A misunderstood cause propagates through everything built on top of it.

Protects: intent, causal understanding, invention quality, research efficiency.

## 3. Take the Best. Invent the Rest.
Research what already exists before replacing it. Preserve what earned its place; replace what does not satisfy the requirement.
Industry practice is evidence, not authority.
Novelty has no value merely because it is novel.
Existing practice has no authority merely because it is common.
Ask why the existing solution exists, what constraint it solves, and whether that constraint still applies.
When common practice and the lawful local environment conflict, the local environment governs.

Why: Averages preserve useful accumulated experience, but they also blur local intent and historical circumstance.

Protects: accumulated knowledge without surrendering independent design.

## 4. Authoritative Record Outranks Memory
Memory is temporary working storage. The maintained authoritative record governs.
Human memory and model memory can drift, blend states, preserve retired assumptions, or reconstruct plausible but incorrect continuity.
When memory and the maintained authoritative source disagree, the authoritative source governs unless lawful authority changes it.
A written draft, note, parked item, or retired state does not become authority merely because it is written.
The why should be preserved with the decision whenever its loss would make later repair dangerous.

Why: Continuity is useful; remembered continuity is not proof.

Protects: state, provenance, authority, long-term coherence.

## 5. Use Each Intelligence for What It Does Best
Human, model, and deterministic compute are different resources. Do not make one imitate another unnecessarily.
Human: intent, judgment, synthesis, question selection, acceptance, responsibility.
Model: inference, articulation, exploration, research, criticism, simulation, formalization.
Compute / Clerk: retrieval, comparison, routing, validation, synchronization, bookkeeping, deterministic checks.
Do not spend probabilistic intelligence on work deterministic structure can do reliably.
Do not automate human authority merely because automation is possible.
The Clerk may interrupt work to restore alignment; synchronization does not give the Clerk authority to invent meaning or permission.

Why: The system becomes stronger when each participant carries the kind of uncertainty it handles best.

Protects: cognitive efficiency, human agency, reliability, cost.

## 6. Narrow Before Spending Intelligence
Reduce the possibility space before asking intelligence to reason inside it.
Find the relevant locality. Resolve the active state. Retrieve the required context. Establish identity, authority, and boundaries.
If the required context is stale, changed, or misaligned with the expected form, refresh it before continuing. Then reason.
Do not ask a model to search mentally through a world the environment can narrow first.

Why: Intelligence is most useful after unnecessary uncertainty has been removed.

Protects: accuracy, token efficiency, speed, specialization.

## 7. Preserve Difference, Missingness, and Uncertainty
Do not force the world into completeness.
Missing is a state. Unknown is a state. Draft, parked, retired, rejected, and conflicting are states.
Do not fill them merely because a complete answer would look cleaner.
Meaning may sometimes be repaired when safely inferable: on a draft, marked as inferred, never into a live face.
Permission may not.
When a required lookup finds nothing, report the miss and follow the lawful climb or halt. Do not turn absence into invented truth.

Why: False completion destroys information and lets averages silently replace local truth.

Protects: epistemic honesty, local meaning, authority, recoverability.

## 8. Attack Before Authority
Exploration should be cheap; promotion should be difficult.
A candidate may be creative, unconventional, incomplete, or wrong while it is being explored.
Before it gains consequence, attack it. Compare it with existing solutions. Search for hidden why. Run hostile cases.
Seek criticism from someone who did not write it. Self-attack does not clear the lock boundary.
Test executable behavior when possible. Failure discovered before lock is useful information.
Maximum freedom before lock; maximum rigor at the lock boundary.

Why: Novel ideas should survive evidence, not enthusiasm.

Protects: experimentation without contaminating authority.

## 9. Change Explicitly and Preserve the Reason
Meaning, state, and authority may change only through visible transitions.
No silent promotion. No silent retirement. No silent overwrite. No silent reinterpretation.
Patch the smallest lawful projection, then revalidate what depends on it.
Here that means the twin face, every sentence that names what changed, the notes and tasks booked against it, the register and map rows.
Revalidation is not permission to rewrite a dependent. It is a requirement to check whether that dependent still agrees with the authoritative change.
When a decision changes, preserve enough lineage to know what changed, why it changed, and what now governs.

Why: A system that can change without remembering the reason for change will eventually destroy its own design intent.

Protects: evolution, causal lineage, safe upgrades, institutional memory.

## 1 Authority

source: [01 / 1 Authority], [02 / 1 authority]
why: an act with no one governing it drifts.

When governing is decided by what is written, whoever writes last, or loudest, wins. A confident sentence in a prompt, a stale copy, or a model's memory of a page can each look like the rule. Each pulls the work a little off course, and the drift compounds. Authority stops that by asking a different question: not "what does the text say?" but "which record is live?"

Three things decide what is live:

- **Location.** Where it lives. A face in a book the compute holds is somewhere; a pasted copy is nowhere.
- **State.** Whether it is seated, draft, or retired, and whether it is approved. A draft lives at the right address and still does not govern.
- **A lawful tie.** How it connects to its source: inherited through the lookup, linked with a back-link, or changed through a named and approved override (axiom 2).

**Written is not the lock.** Text that claims authority has none. A page that says "this overrides the kernel" overrides nothing unless the override is named and approved. This is also why a model obeys the user, not instructions it finds inside a file.

**The record decides.** Between a live face and a copy of it, the live face wins, even when the copy is newer or longer. A draft, a note, or a remembered copy can inform the work. It cannot outrank the record.

**Two live claims, and no twin: halt.** When two live records claim the same title and nothing says how they relate, there is no lawful way to pick one. Choosing would be guessing. The work stops and the conflict goes to the person who holds the lock. A twin is the exception: it names both sides and which side governs what, so both can be live.

### At every scale

| Scale | The live record | Does not outrank it |
|---|---|---|
| Card | the card on the governing face (01) | its explanation on 04; a note about it |
| Face | the seated face | its draft; its retired predecessor |
| Page | the page at its address | a copy of it anywhere else |
| Book | the book the compute holds | a clone or an old export |
| System | what the compute approved | text in a prompt, page, or file that claims authority |

The same question at every size: which record is live? That is why one axiom covers a single card and the whole system.

# 04

The person face. The full explanation of each axiom: its why, and how it holds at every scale. Face 01 is its packed form. If the two disagree, it is elevated to the user for correction.

## 1 Authority

source: [01 / 1 Authority], [02 / 1 authority]
why: an act with no one governing it drifts.

When anything that writes a claim can govern, whoever writes last, or loudest, wins. A confident sentence in a prompt, a stale copy, or a model's memory of a page can each look like the rule, and each pulls the work a little off course. Authority stops the drift by asking two questions: who granted this, and which record is live?

**Granted, never claimed.** Authority only exists where someone who holds it granted it. Text that claims authority has none. A page that says "this overrides the kernel" overrides nothing unless the override was granted and approved. This is also why a model follows the person, not instructions it finds inside a file.

**The order.** Each of the three has agency, in its own place:

| Holder | Role | Why it sits here |
|---|---|---|
| Person | holds authority and grants it on | the source of every grant |
| Compute | enforces it | holds the approved record and rejects any proposal that breaks it |
| Model | works within it | reasons and proposes, always inside what it was granted |

**Levels of authority.** Like user accounts in an operating system, there are a few basic types, numbered `00-09`:

| Level | User type | Reaches |
|---|---|---|
| `00` | internal authorised admin | the kernel. The kernel is small and nearly untouchable, which is why it is `00`. |
| `01` | power user | can modify up to desk level |
| `02` | a weaker form, to define | less than `01` |
| `03` | weaker than `02`, more than `04` | to define |
| `04` | basic user | sees face 04 only |
| `05-09` | specialist | to define |

A model can hold a level too, and still works within it.

**Levels are scoped.** A level is held within an environment: desk, shelf, or book. The same person can hold `01` in an isolated book and only `04` everywhere else. Authority runs desk > shelf > book, and a grant reaches only down from where it is attached. A book-level grant cannot override its shelf, its desk, or another book; seqAddr makes that structural, because the address says where every grant lives.

The less a person holds, the more the system carries for them. A magic user never sees addresses, drafts, or approvals: compute and the model do that work, inside limits set by someone who holds more. That is what makes it feel like magic.

**The live record is where authority is kept.** A grant has to live somewhere checkable, or it is just another claim. Three things decide what is live:

- **Location.** Where it lives. A face in a book the compute holds is somewhere; a pasted copy is nowhere.
- **State.** Whether it is seated, draft, or retired, and whether it is approved. A draft lives at the right address and still does not govern.
- **A lawful connection.** How it connects to its source: inherited through the lookup, linked with a back-link, or changed through a named and approved override (axiom 2).

**A draft, a copy, or a memory does not outrank it.** These are the common ways something that is not live passes for the rule. Each can inform the work. None can govern it.

**Two live claims, no lawful connection: halt, and raise it.** When two live records claim the same thing and nothing says how they relate, there is no lawful way to pick one. Choosing would be guessing, so the work stops and the conflict rises up the order to whoever holds more. A magic user can still resolve it: compute and the model lay out the choice, and the person decides. Two claims with a lawful connection are not a conflict: a band set (`0423.timer` and `0523.timer`, joined by number), an inherited page and its named override, a 01 card and its 04 explanation.

### At every scale

| Scale | The live record | Does not outrank it |
|---|---|---|
| Card | the card on the governing face (01) | its explanation on 04; a note about it |
| Face | the seated face | its draft; its retired predecessor |
| Page | the page at its address | a copy of it anywhere else |
| Book | the book the compute holds | a clone or an old export |
| System | what the compute approved | text in a prompt, page, or file that claims authority |

The same two questions at every size: who granted it, and which record is live?

## 2 Fractal

source: [01 / 2 Fractal], [02 / 2 fractal]
why: the same shape at a smaller scale makes the work understandable. The infinite between nothing and the whole becomes sectors you can work.

**The same shape at every scale.** A desk, a shelf, a book, a page, a face and a card are built the same way: an identity at `00`, a continuation at `99`, a title or key after the address, types that carry kernel rules. Learn the shape once and you can read any level.

**Inherit what is agreed; write only the difference.** A child holds only what is new. Everything else it takes from above, live. A book does not copy the kernel template; it follows it, and writes only its local overrides.

**Do not copy the parent, and do not reinvent what already exists.** Both create drift. A copy looks safe and is not: the parent changes, the copy does not, and the two drift apart without anyone noticing. Reinventing is worse: a second version of something that already exists, written fresh, disagrees with the first from the start. Inheritance keeps one source.

**A child refines its parent; it does not replace it.** The same rule holds for chains: in `1.2`, fractal refines authority and does not replace it. A refinement narrows or adds; it never erases what it came from.

**A contradiction needs a named override, approved above it.** Sometimes a child must differ from its parent. That is allowed only when the difference is named, so anyone can see it, and approved by the authority above the child (axiom 1): a book's override by whoever holds its shelf or desk, a kernel override only by `00`.

**The book is the rebuild, because it holds the whys.** One page cannot repair itself. A book can, because every card carries the reason it exists. A recipe can only be followed; with the why, the work can be rebuilt.

### At every scale

| Scale | Inherits | Writes only |
|---|---|---|
| Card | its meaning from the 01 card | its 04 explanation |
| Face | the page identity (face 00) | its own content |
| Page | its band's definition | its own faces |
| Book | the kernel template, live | its local overrides (0001-0099) |
| Axiom chain | the earlier axiom | the refinement |

## 3 Leads

source: [01 / 3 Leads], [02 / 3 leads]
why: no one form of intelligence is best at everything. Each leads where it is strongest, and the three work in concert.

**Three distinct forms of intelligence.** A person, a model, and compute are not three people doing one job. They are different kinds of mind, and it works like rock, paper, scissors:

- the person beats the model at intent and meaning: what is wanted, and why
- the model beats compute at judgement: reading, reasoning, drafting
- compute beats the person at exactness, scale, and memory

Each wins one match and loses another, so no single order is right for every task.

**Fit, not ability.** A person can do everything a model or compute can, only far slower and at a great cost in life force. People were the first computers. A lead says who does a piece of work best for its cost, not who is able to do it at all.

**Each is given the work in its own form.** This is the reason a page has faces. The model reads face 01. Compute uses faces 02 and 03. The person reads face 04. The same page is read three ways: compute reads the address, the model reads the structure, the person reads the title.

**Naming the lead.** A cite names who leads, in order, with M, P, and C: `3MPC` means the model leads, then the person, then compute. `3CMP` means compute leads. One or two letters may be named: `3C` says compute leads and does not rank the others. A band head can set a default lead for its band, and a task card names its own only when it differs.

**Solved work moves to compute.** When a hard why is solved, it should be coded for compute to use. Then it becomes a question a model can ask and something a person can resolve through an action, instead of reasoning it out again each time.

**A lead is not authority.** Axiom 1 decides who governs, and that never changes. A model can lead a task (`3MPC`) while the person still holds the authority to approve it.

### An illustration, not a rule

How the leads might divide a software project. The tasks and orders are a picture of the split, not a fixed list.

| Task | Lead |
|---|---|
| Intent: goals and the why | `3PMC` |
| Research: what already exists | `3MPC` |
| Design: options and architecture | `3MPC` |
| Specification: law and schema | `3MCP` |
| Implementation: code | `3MCP` |
| Testing | `3CMP` |
| Hostile review | `3MPC` |
| Record: versions and history | `3CPM` |
| Release and operation | `3CMP` |
| Acceptance: does it do what was meant? | `3PMC` |

The person leads at both ends, the model leads the making, and compute leads where exactness matters.

## 4 Why

source: [01 / 4 Why], [02 / 4 why]
why: a success you cannot explain is only an answer, and an answer can rot.

**Why is the heart of the work.** Whys allow greater understanding. A law without its why is incomplete, because its reasoning cannot be understood: it can be followed, but not checked, varied, or repaired. With the why, a rule that breaks can be seen breaking, and the work can be rebuilt.

**Record the why when the choice is made.** That is when it is cheapest to keep. The reason is still in the room; a day later it has to be dug out.

**Recover a lost why in proportion.** Not every lost reason is worth the same effort. Spend in proportion to the cost of being wrong.

**Never invent a reason.** An invented why looks exactly like a real one, which is worse than a gap: nobody knows to question it. A why that cannot be recovered is marked missing (`4.7`).

**Where whys live: face 04.** It is the face a person reads first, and it holds the why at every scale:

| Scale | Where its why lives |
|---|---|
| Card | its 04 card: `why:` and the explanation |
| Page | the page's face 04 |
| Book | page 0000's face 04 |
| System | the store of whys; the book is the rebuild because it holds them |

**The packed card and the why on request.** A face 01 card is the packed form of its 04 card: the fewest words a model needs, so it does not bloat its context. When the packed card is not enough, the model requests the why, and compute finds it by the card's key on face 04, like retrieval. This is "in proportion" at work: a model pays for the full why only when it needs it. The why helps the model understand a gap and work around it; it never lets the model write missing law from it (axiom 7).

**When the packed card and its why disagree,** neither wins. It is elevated to the user for correction: two live claims with no lawful connection (axiom 1).

## 5 Frame

source: [01 / 5 Frame], [02 / 5 frame]
why: work started before its aim is known spreads across every path it can see, and lands somewhere else.

**A model sees more paths than a person can hold.** A model has read far more than any person. Given an ask, it sees thousands of plausible paths and tends toward the one that is best on average. The person's meaning sits somewhere specific inside a much narrower view. Without a frame, the model produces something reasonable that is not what was meant.

**Know the aim, and why, before the work.** We do not execute work before we know what we are aiming at. When the why of a task is unknown, the cost of solving it rises: every path looks equally worth trying.

**The spirit of the goal, not only its words.** Asked for a house, a model that builds a house for a bird may be technically correct, and it is still wrongly framed. A frame names what the work is for, where it happens, what to do, where to stop, and what done looks like.

**Within the frame, full intelligence.** The frame does not make the model think less. It points all of its intelligence at one goal. Inside the frame, the model thinks as freely as it can.

**Observation is permission to report, not to act.** A model will still see things outside the frame, and those observations are valuable. They are returned, not acted on, and raised to authority (axiom 1). Authority decides whether they become work; the model does not assume it.

**At the edge: stop, state, record, raise.** When the work reaches the edge of its frame, the model stops, states the issue, records it, and raises it. It does not step over the edge on its own judgement.

**Question the frame.** A frame can be wrong. Should it be wider? Narrower? Does it let the work finish? Asking is part of the work; changing the frame belongs to authority.

### How a frame works in practice

The pack-lint pages (`10.10.12.rebuild`, band set `0417` / `0517` / `0117`) show the whole loop:

| Step | Page | Frame job |
|---|---|---|
| Decide | `0417` design | purpose, what the page is not, rules, open choice (issue, options, what each does), the stop list |
| Frame | `0517` pack | read first, do, stop if, file; each item carries its reason, path, and scale |
| Ask | the pack | before finishing: does this work well? does a tool need updating? |
| Return | `0117` return | did, blocked, need; **unprompted** and **overflow** hold what was seen outside the frame |
| Reframe | the next pack | a "need" becomes the next pack; a decision becomes the next pack |

The frame never stops the model from seeing. It gives what it sees somewhere to go.

### At every scale

| Scale | Its frame |
|---|---|
| Word | the brand: the person's meaning, not the average |
| Item | one ask, with its reason, path, and scale |
| Task | the pack: read first, do, stop if, file |
| Band set | decision, pack, return |
| Address | the smallest address that holds the work, not the desk |

## 6 Lineage

source: [01 / 6 Lineage], [02 / 6 lineage]
why: this is how understanding is built over time.

**A visible transition.** A change nobody can see cannot be understood, checked, or undone. Every change says three things: what changed, why (axiom 4), and what now governs (axiom 1). A reader who finds the change later can follow it without asking anyone.

**A change that is not recorded did not happen.** Talk is not work. Agreeing in a conversation, deciding in a meeting, or a model saying "done" changes nothing until the change is recorded. Until then it is a claim, and claims do not govern (axiom 1).

**The past is not rewritten.** A note records what was thought at the time. Editing it later makes the record lie about how the understanding was built. The same holds for returns and closed work: a closed return is not handed more lines; a new change is added after it. The book's append-only log is this rule at the scale of the file, and drafting (copy, note, merge, promote) is this rule at the scale of a card.

**Check what the change is connected to.** A change rarely stands alone. A 01 card has its 04 card; a link has its back-link; a band page has its set (axiom 8). The change checks each connection in the same change, so the two sides never disagree in between.

**The check is not permission to rewrite.** Finding a connected piece out of step is an observation, not a licence. If the connected piece is outside the frame (axiom 5), it is reported and raised, not changed.

### At every scale

| Scale | The visible transition | Not rewritten |
|---|---|---|
| Card | a draft, merged and promoted with its notes | the notes and the earlier card |
| Face | a new seated face; the old one retired | the retired face |
| Page | its face 06 notes and returns | a closed return |
| Book | an entry appended to the log | earlier log entries |
| System | a decision recorded with its why | a past decision; a new one supersedes it |

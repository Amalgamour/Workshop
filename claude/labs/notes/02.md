## Chain probe

Four cold reads of ten chains (`returns/02.probe.*`), given the full Axioms.md and nothing else.

- **All four read order as meaningful**: the first number is the lens, and later numbers refine it. The writer should put first what the reader must settle first. This matches the original kernel's "primary identifies, secondary composes, tertiary qualifies", which none of them were shown.
- `4.7` was read as "a why is missing" by all four. `7.8` as "a connection is missing" by all four. `7.4` as "something is missing; ask why", the same parts with the gap first.
- **`8.7` split two ways:** "a connection that is itself incomplete" (two runs) and "a pointer exists but its target is missing" (two runs). So `7.8` and `8.7` are not interchangeable to a cold reader.
- **Haiku 1 found the inconsistency unprompted:** "`4.7` is described as 'a why' (missing after the subject) while `7.8` is described as 'a connection' (missing before the subject). … The pack should fix one rule for `7`." This is the open question from axiom 7, found cold.
- `2.5` and `5.2` were read as fractal-then-frame and frame-then-fractal, consistently. `3CM` was read as compute (a tool) leads, by all four.

**For the Director:** the chain grammar holds, and the meaning of order transfers. One decision is needed: where `7` goes. Two consistent options:
1. **`7` always last** (`4.7` a missing why, `8.7` a missing connection, `1.7` authority missing a piece). Then `7.8` would need rewriting as `8.7`, which changes your example.
2. **`7` first means "something is missing, of this kind"** (`7.8`, `7.4`), **and `7` last means "this thing is incomplete"** (`8.7`, `4.7`). Both are allowed, and they mean different things. This matches how the cold readers split on `8.7`.

## What lab 02 says

1. **The axioms help, and the packed form helps at least as much as the full text.** Passes: A 16, B 21, C 24 of 32. The sample is small.
2. **The weaker model gains most in protection.** Haiku without axioms rewrote history, closed a gap by renumbering, removed a code and broke inheritance. Haiku with either form of the axioms did none of these.
3. **Fractal (2) and Leads (3) showed the clearest behavioural differences**: differences-only pages and named overrides, and naming compute for the allocation.

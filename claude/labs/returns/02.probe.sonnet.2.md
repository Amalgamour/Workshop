## Last question

Yes, the order changes what a chain tells you. The axioms say a dot orders them: `1.7.8` reads as authority, then missing, then connection. A later axiom refines the earlier one rather than replacing it. So `4.7` is a why that is missing, seen through the why. `7.4` starts from the hole and asks about the why behind it. The first number sets the governing concern, and each later number narrows it.

To decide what goes first when writing one, put first the axiom that governs the reader's next move, meaning the question the reader must settle before the others matter. Usually that is authority (1) when permission is in doubt, or the frame (5) when scope is in doubt. Then add the refinements in the order the reader should apply them. If I cannot say why one number leads, I should record that why (4) and not guess. For `3`, the letter order is also meaningful: it names who leads first.

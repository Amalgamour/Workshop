# Probe 03: reading chains

1. `7.8`: A connection is missing. The writer wants the next reader to know that a link they expected cannot be opened. The reader should not repair it from memory or from a similar-looking piece, should work around the hole, and should report it.

2. `8.7`: A connection exists but is incomplete. The writer is warning that one end of the link is unfinished. The reader should use only what is written, not assume the other side, and raise the gap rather than invent the missing part.

3. `4.7`: A why is missing. The writer wants the next reader to recover the reason, but only in proportion to the cost of being wrong and never by inventing one. The reader should look for the why in what is written, ask for it if the cost is high, and otherwise leave it missing and report it.

4. `7.4`: Something is missing and its why is needed to understand the gap. The writer wants the reader to ask why it is missing before trying to solve it. The reader should not fill it without written permission; if the work is within the frame, report it.

5. `6.8`: Lineage, then connection. The writer is pointing to a piece whose line must be traced: back to what governs it and forward to what depends on it, using only written connections. The reader should trace that line, and if the trail is stale, raise it without rewriting any step.

6. `8.6`: Connection, then lineage. The writer is saying the link to this piece is written, but its line of descent must also be followed. The reader should confirm the connection is written, follow the lineage, and record any change as a new step rather than rewriting the past.

7. `9.5`: Trial, then frame. The piece has not yet faced its hostile trial, so it should not be trusted as it stands. The reader should test it against the frame, meaning what the work aims at, where it stops, and what done looks like, and compare it with its predecessor before promoting or replacing anything.

8. `2.6`: Fractal, then lineage. The piece inherits what its parent already agrees on and records only the difference. The reader should not copy the parent or reinvent what exists, and should trace the difference back to its parent so the line stays intact.

9. `1.7.9`: Authority, then missing, then trial. The piece has not been shown to have authority, has a gap, and has not been tested. The reader should not act on it until authority is confirmed, the gap is handled with permission or reported, and the piece has faced its trial.

10. `3C`: Compute leads. The writer is assigning the work mainly to compute, in its own form (mechanical, deterministic work). The reader should give the task to compute in that form, keep the person and model roles for the parts they lead, and remember that the lead is not authority.

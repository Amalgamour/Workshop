# Probe 02: reading chains

1. `4.7`: The writer means a why is missing. The reader should recover the why in proportion to the cost of being wrong, never invent one, and report the gap if they lack permission to fill it.

2. `7.4`: Read as "missing, then why": something is missing and the reader should ask why it is missing before doing anything else. Ambiguity: the axioms only give `4.7` as "a why" and `7.8` as "a connection", so the exact meaning of a 7-led chain with 4 is inferred, not stated. The reader should ask why the gap exists, not search for it.

3. `7.8`: A connection is missing across desks, books, pages or cards. The reader should not invent the link, should work around the hole using what is written, and should report it.

4. `8.7`: A connection is present as a pointer, and the thing it points to is missing. The reader should check the connection, report the missing target, and not fabricate it.

5. `1.7`: Authority is missing or unclear. The reader should not act on it, should find out who holds authority, and should raise it to whoever holds more. If two live claims conflict, halt.

6. `7.1`: Something is missing, and the authority over it is what matters. The reader should ask whether they have written permission to solve the gap. Without it, leave the gap and raise it. Do not fill it from memory.

7. `2.5`: Fractal first, then frame. The reader should inherit what the parent already agrees, write only the difference, and check that the difference sits within the frame of the goal and its stopping point.

8. `5.2`: Frame first, then fractal. The reader should confirm the frame (aim, scope, stop point, done) before applying the parent's shape to the child.

9. `1.7.8`: Authority, then missing, then connection. This is the cold set before a write. The reader should confirm authority on the live record, note what is missing, and check connections, all before writing. Halt if live claims conflict.

10. `3CM`: Compute leads, then model. The reader should give the computable part of the work to compute first, then the model's judgment. The lead is not authority.

## Last question

Yes, the order changes what the chain says. Dot order ranks the pieces: the first number is the one that governs or must be resolved first, and later numbers refine it. Compare `1.7` (authority is missing, so nothing else can be trusted yet) with `7.1` (something is missing, and its authority is the question). To decide which number goes first, ask what the reader must settle before the others make sense. Authority comes before missing, and missing comes before connection, as the cold set `1.7.8` shows. For leads (`3MPC`), the first letter is the one that leads the work.

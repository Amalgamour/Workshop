# Probe 03: reading chains

1. `7.8`
The writer meant that a connection is missing: a link the piece needs cannot be opened. The next reader should read what is written, work around the hole, and not repair the link from memory or from a similar piece. Report it and raise it; fill it only with written permission, as a marked draft.

2. `8.7`
The writer meant that a connection exists but is incomplete: part of it, such as one side of a link or its back-link, is missing. The next reader should follow only the written part, treat anything beyond the gap as unconfirmed, and not guess the rest. Note the gap and raise it if the work requires it.

3. `4.7`
The writer meant that a why is missing. The next reader should not invent a reason so the work feels finished. They should try to recover the why in proportion to the cost of being wrong, and if it cannot be recovered from a source, leave it missing and report it. (Ordering note: the axioms define `4.7` as "a why" missing, so the 4 marks the why and the 7 marks it as absent.)

4. `7.4`
The writer meant that something is incomplete, and the order puts the missing mark before the why. The next reader should recognise the hole first, then ask why it is missing before filling anything, and not fill it from invention. (Ordering note: this reads as "missing, then consider its why", the reverse of `4.7`. The pack does not define this chain directly, so this reading is inferred from the dot-order rule.)

5. `6.8`
The writer meant that a piece's lineage must be travelled through written connections. The next reader should follow the line back to what governs the piece and forward to what depends on it, using only written links and not matching content. If the trail is stale, raise it, and do not rewrite the past; any change is added as a new step.

6. `8.6`
The writer meant that a connection comes first and the lineage follows from it. The next reader should open the written connection, then follow the lineage it sits on to what governs the piece. Any change is recorded as a new step, and a stale trail is raised.

7. `9.5`
The writer meant that the piece has not yet faced its trial, and the frame itself must also be questioned. The next reader should not trust or promote the piece as it stands. Before replacing anything they should open the predecessor read-only and run a hostile pass, and they should check whether the frame is the right size and lets the work finish.

8. `2.6`
The writer meant that the piece inherits what is agreed from its parent and writes only the difference, with that difference recorded in its lineage. The next reader should not copy the parent or reinvent what exists. They should write the difference, trace it to its parent, and if it contradicts the parent, require a named override approved by the authority above.

9. `1.7.9`
The writer meant, in order: confirm authority, note what is missing, then account for the trial. The next reader should first establish who grants authority over the piece and halt and raise it if two live claims have no lawful connection. They should leave any missing part unfilled without written permission, and treat the piece as untrusted until it has faced its trial.

10. `3C`
The writer meant that compute leads this part of the work. The next reader should give the work in compute form, such as calculation, enumeration, or checking, while person and model keep their own parts. The lead is not authority.

# Probe 02 return

1. `4.7` — The writer is saying a why is missing: the reason behind a choice was never recorded. The reader should not invent one; ask why it is missing, and if there is no written permission to fill it, leave it missing and report it (recover it in proportion to the cost of being wrong).

2. `7.4` — Read in order, this starts from the hole and then asks for its why: something is incomplete, and the reader should first ask why it is missing. It is close to `4.7`, but the emphasis is on the gap first, with the why used to understand it. The reader should leave it missing, not invent it, and report it if it is within the frame.

3. `7.8` — A connection is missing: a pointer between cards, pages, desks or books is absent. The reader should work around the hole, not search for or invent the link, and report it if it is within the frame and nothing requires it.

4. `8.7` — Reading 8 as a connection, this says that a connection exists but is incomplete. It is a pointer that is not finished. The reader should follow what is written, treat the rest as unknown, and not complete it without permission. (8 is proposed and not yet scrubbed, so this reading is provisional.)

5. `1.7` — Authority is missing or unclear: the grant of who may decide, or permission to solve a hole, is absent. The reader should not claim authority or fill the gap; they should leave it missing and raise it to whoever holds more authority.

6. `7.1` — A gap exists and the writer is pointing at authority as the question. The reader should check whether they have written permission to solve it. Without that permission, leave it and report it. It is the same ingredients as `1.7`, but it starts from the hole and then asks about permission.

7. `2.5` — Inherit what is agreed (fractal), then work within the frame. The writer means to tell the reader to write only the difference from the parent, and to know the aim and edges of the work. The reader should use the parent's agreed form, stay inside the frame, and report anything outside it instead of acting on it.

8. `5.2` — Frame first, then fractal. The reader should first fix what the work aims at, why, where it stops and what done looks like, and then apply the inherited shape, writing only the difference. The frame sets the scope within which inheritance is applied.

9. `1.7.8` — The cold set before a write: authority, then missing, then connection. The reader should check who granted authority and which record is live, then notice what is missing and ask whether they have permission to fill it, then check what it is connected to. They should not choose between two conflicting live claims, and should raise them instead.

10. `3CM` — Leads: compute leads, then the model. A modifier on axiom 3 only. The reader should give compute the part of the work it does best (checking, enforcing, counting), have the model take the next part, and understand that a lead is not authority and permits nothing.

## Last question

Yes, the order changes what a chain tells you. The dot says "then", so the first number is the starting lens and later numbers refine it (a later axiom refines, it does not replace). `4.7` starts from why and asks what is missing about it, while `7.4` starts from the hole and asks why. `1.7.8` is a sequence of checks done in that order. A comma (`1,3`) splits pieces and carries no order of that kind. In `3CM` the letter order is also meaningful, since it names who leads first.

To decide which number goes first when writing one, I would put first the thing the reader must settle before the others make sense. Usually that is the lens or gate the reader must pass through first (authority before anything else, or the frame before inheritance), and what the card is mainly about comes after as the refinement. If a hole is the main subject, I would lead with 7 only when the reader should notice the gap before anything else, and otherwise start from the axiom that governs how to handle it. When two orders are equally plausible, I would ask the reader to decide by the question "what would go wrong if they read the other first?" and put first the one whose absence is costlier. If I cannot tell, I would not guess; I would say the order is unresolved and raise it.

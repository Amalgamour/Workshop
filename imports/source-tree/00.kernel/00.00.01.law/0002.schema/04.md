## Job

source: [01 / Job](01.md#job); [02 / Job](02.md#job)

why: A short usable form can preserve a rule without pretending that all compact forms serve the same job.

Face 02 supplies a compact form of a named subject section: a card, template, or schema appropriate to that subject. It does not add normative conditions absent from face 01. These forms are named separately rather than treating every box as interchangeable.

The compact form selects the useful handle: section: <heading>; source: <face 01 section>; form: card | template | schema.

## Coverage

source: [01 / Coverage](01.md#coverage); [02 / Coverage](02.md#coverage)

why: This prevents a template requirement from becoming compulsory duplication.

Create a face 02 section when a compact representation serves the job. Its source section is explicit. A card that is shorter than the rule stays. A card whose only extra sentence is to read the source comes off. If no compact form is needed, its face is absent and the menu omits it.

The compact form selects the useful handle: Useful compact form -> supply it. Unneeded face -> omit it.

## Agreement

source: [01 / Agreement](01.md#agreement); [02 / Agreement](02.md#agreement)

why: Alignment is meaning preserved across forms, not matching text or matching headings alone.

Preserve the governing conditions, exceptions, and limits in the compact form. A card that changes a condition is a conflict even when its heading matches. Report that conflict with both source cuts. Read the actual governing file before a consequential write. Governing conditions, exceptions, and limits outrank shortness. A card may be shorter. A shorter card that drops a limit or adds a condition is a conflict. Shortness does not win.

The compact form selects the useful handle: Same conditions, different form. Disagreement -> finding, retain both. Conditions, exceptions, and limits outrank shortness. A dropped limit or an added condition is a conflict.
